Step 1 — Presenting Features

Why this matters:
PE can be subtle and is frequently missed. Sudden dyspnoea or pleuritic pain without a clear alternative diagnosis should prompt PE consideration.

Step 2 — Immediate Stability

Red flag:
Haemodynamic instability suggests massive PE → immediate senior review, resus approach, urgent imaging and/or treatment pathway.

Step 3 — Wells Criteria

Why this matters:
Wells stratifies pre-test probability and guides investigation (e.g., D-dimer vs direct imaging).

PERC Rule (auto): Complete ONLY if Wells is low risk.
Why this matters:
If low risk AND PERC negative, PE is considered sufficiently unlikely that further testing may be avoided (context-dependent).

Step 5 — Investigations

  • ECG (often sinus tachycardia; consider right heart strain patterns)
  • CXR (rule out alternative causes; usually non-specific in PE)
  • D-dimer if low/intermediate probability and appropriate
  • CTPA if high probability or D-dimer positive
  • Troponin/BNP if risk stratifying (RV strain)
  • ABG/VBG if hypoxic or unwell

Step 6 — Management

  • Oxygen only if hypoxic
  • Analgesia
  • Anticoagulation unless contraindicated (consider bleeding risk)
  • If unstable: senior/ICU, consider thrombolysis / interventional options per local protocol
DOAC selection + dosing (educational — local protocol + renal function required):
Standard VTE treatment regimens (adults) — confirm contraindications, drug interactions, pregnancy status, renal/hepatic impairment.
  • Apixaban: 10 mg BD x 7 days, then 5 mg BD (extended phase often 2.5 mg BD if indicated)
  • Rivaroxaban: 15 mg BD x 21 days, then 20 mg daily (with food)
Note: Avoid/seek specialist advice in pregnancy, severe renal impairment, significant liver disease, extremes of weight, or if strong interacting medicines. Consider heparin/warfarin pathways where appropriate.

Step 7 — Disposition (ED vs Outpatient)

ED admission / higher-acuity pathway triggers:
  • Shock / hypotension, persistent hypoxia, syncope
  • Evidence of RV strain (echo/CT), raised troponin, significant tachycardia
  • High clinical probability, major comorbidities, high bleeding risk
  • Inadequate supports / inability to adhere / follow-up not assured
Possible outpatient pathway (site-dependent):
Low-risk PE, stable vitals, low bleeding risk, reliable follow-up, clear safety net.
