Primary Dysmenorrhoea – Australian GP Click-Through Pathway

Initial Presentation

Patient presents with recurrent lower abdominal pain associated with menstruation.

Step 1 – Is this likely Primary Dysmenorrhoea?

  • Onset within 6–12 months of menarche
  • Cyclic crampy lower abdominal pain
  • Pain starts just before or at onset of menses
  • No pelvic pathology symptoms

Step 2 – Screen for Red Flags (Secondary Causes)

  • Onset after age 25
  • Progressively worsening pain
  • Pain outside menstruation
  • Dyspareunia
  • Heavy or irregular bleeding
  • Infertility
  • Poor response to NSAIDs + hormonal therapy

If red flags present → investigate / refer.

Step 3 – First-Line Non-Pharmacological Management

  • Heat therapy (heat pack / hot water bottle)
  • Regular aerobic exercise
  • Stretching, yoga, relaxation techniques
  • Lifestyle advice: sleep, stress, reduce caffeine/smoking
  • TENS therapy (if available)

Step 4 – First-Line Pharmacological Treatment

  • NSAIDs (first line)
    • Ibuprofen
    • Naproxen
    • Mefenamic acid
  • Start 1–2 days before menses or at onset of pain
  • Paracetamol if NSAIDs contraindicated

Step 5 – Hormonal Therapy (If NSAIDs Inadequate)

  • Combined oral contraceptive pill (cyclic or continuous)
  • Progestogen-only pill
  • Levonorgestrel IUD (e.g. Mirena)
  • Depot medroxyprogesterone (selected cases)

Step 6 – Review & Escalation

  • Assess response after 2–3 cycles
  • If inadequate response → consider pelvic exam
  • Pelvic ultrasound if indicated
  • Refer to gynaecology if refractory or red flags

Consider endometriosis if persistent symptoms.
