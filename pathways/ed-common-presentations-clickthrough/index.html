Emergency Department Decision Tool

Common ED Presentations — Structured Clinical Reasoning

Step 1 — Select Presentation

Why this matters:
ED presentations often appear benign but may conceal life-threatening pathology.

Step 2 — Immediate Risk Assessment

Always assess for:
  • Airway compromise
  • Respiratory distress
  • Circulatory instability
  • Altered consciousness
  • Severe pain out of proportion

Step 3 — Focused Red Flags

High-risk features to actively exclude:
  • Chest pain → ACS, PE, dissection
  • Headache → SAH, meningitis
  • Abdominal pain → peritonitis, AAA
  • Dyspnoea → PE, pneumonia, heart failure
  • Mental health → suicidality, psychosis

Step 4 — Initial Investigations

Common first-line ED investigations:
  • ECG
  • Bloods (FBE, U&E, CRP, troponin)
  • Imaging (CXR, CT where indicated)
  • Urinalysis
  • ABG / VBG if unwell

Step 5 — Initial Management

Principles:
  • Analgesia early
  • Oxygen only if hypoxic
  • IV access if unstable
  • Antibiotics if sepsis suspected
  • Early senior review if red flags
